Das Genus deutscher Nomen — Regeln statt Raten.
Twenty-three suffixes decide the gender of most German nouns. Learn those, and der/die/das stops being a lottery.
8 feminine · 8 masculine · 7 neuter

Twenty-three endings. That is the whole core of the system. Everything else in this trainer is either a scene you can picture, a structural shortcut, or a short list of traps. Your textbook says „Dafür gibt es keine Regeln" (“there are no rules for that”) — true only for short, ending-less nouns like der Baum, das Herz, die Maus. Those are the minority.

Der Trichter — the four gates

Run every unknown noun through these gates in order. Stop at the first one that answers. The order matters: gate 1 overrides gate 2, and gate 2 overrides gate 3 — that is why das Mädchen (“the girl”) is neuter although it is a female person.

  1. Is it a compound noun?

    Only the last element carries the gender. The words in front only add meaning. One known noun unlocks hundreds.

    die Tageszeitung ← die Zeitung · das Bewerbungsgespräch ← das Gespräch · der Hauptbahnhof ← der Hof

  2. Does it end in one of the 23?

    The suffix beats meaning, always. This gate decides the large majority of B2 vocabulary, because abstract and academic nouns are almost all built with suffixes.

    die Entscheidung · der Praktikant · das Monument

  3. Does it belong to one of the three scenes?

    Semantic families: people, time, weather, compass points, alcohol → the storm scene. Small things, imports, verbs-turned-nouns → the museum. Female persons and abstractions → the pastry shop.

    der Montag · der Schnee · das Hotel · die Frau

  4. Nothing? Then play the odds.

    Ends in -e → die (~90%). Bare verb stem, no ending → der. Verb stem + -t → die. Infinitive used as a noun → das. If all else fails, guess die — it is the most frequent gender in German.

    die Suppe · der Besuch (besuchen) · die Fahrt (fahren) · das Leben (leben)

Die drei Welten — three scenes, three genders

Do not memorise the semantic rules as a list. Memorise three pictures. Every noun inside a picture has that picture's gender, so recalling one image recalls six or seven rules at once.

derDer Mann im Sturm8 Endungen

Ein Mann steht im Norden am See. Es ist Montag, der dritte März. Ein Sturm kommt, der Regen fällt. Er trinkt einen Whisky und wartet.

A man stands in the north by the lake. It is Monday, the third of March. A storm is coming, the rain is falling. He drinks a whisky and waits.

Everything in the scene is masculine: the man, the compass point, the day, the month, the storm, the rain, the whisky. Male persons and professions · days, months, seasons, parts of the day · weather · compass points · alcoholic drinks.

-er-ling-ist-ant -ent-eur-or-ismus

Der = the Doer. Seven of these eight endings name a person who does something — der Veranstalter, der Lehrling, der Komponist, der Praktikant, der Student, der Regisseur, der Direktor — and the eighth names an ideology: der Terrorismus. Also masculine: bare verb stems with no ending (der Besuch ← besuchen) and many nouns in -et (der Planet).

dasDas Kind im Museum7 Endungen

Ein Mädchen steht im Museum. Es liest ein Dokument über ein Thema, isst ein Sandwich und findet alles Schöne interessant. Das Essen danach ist im Hotel.

A girl stands in the museum. She reads a document about a topic, eats a sandwich, and finds everything beautiful interesting. The meal afterwards is at the hotel.

Two families live here: things made small (any noun in -chen/-lein, whatever it means) and things imported or converted — Latin and Greek loans, international words, verbs and adjectives turned into nouns.

-chen-lein-um -ma-ment-ing-tum

Das = the Small, the Latin, the Converted. -chen is the strongest rule in the whole language: it overrides meaning without exception — das Mädchen (“the girl”), das Kaninchen (“the rabbit”). Plus infinitives as nouns (das Leben, das Treffen), adjectives as nouns (das Schöne, das Beste), most -nis nouns (das Ergebnis) and most international words (das Auto, das Foto, das Kino, das Telefon).

dieDie Frau in der Konditorei8 Endungen

Eine Frau führt eine Konditorei. Eine Kundin bezahlt die Rechnung. Die Entscheidung bringt ihr Zufriedenheit, die Freundschaft mit den Gästen Sicherheit.

A woman runs a pastry shop. A customer pays the bill. The decision brings her satisfaction; the friendship with the guests brings security.

This is where German keeps its abstractions. Nearly every noun the language builds out of a verb or an adjective to name an action, a quality or a state is feminine — which is exactly the vocabulary a B2 text is made of.

-ung-heit-keit-schaft -ion-tät-ik-ur

Say it as a rhythm — two bars of four. ung · heit · keit · schaft — ion · tät · ik · ur. These eight are close to 100% reliable; the only common exception in the set is das Abitur. Also feminine: female persons, -in (die Kundin), -ei (die Bäckerei), -enz/-anz (die Kompetenz), -ie (die Sinfonie), most nouns in -e (die Suppe), and verb stem + -t (die Fahrt ← fahren).

Zwei Gratis-Regeln — two rules that cost nothing

Every plural is dieGender only exists in the singular. In the plural all three genders collapse into one form.der Mann → die Männer · das Kind → die Kinder
Compounds inherit from the rightThe last element is the head; everything before it is a modifier. Test it by deleting the front part.die Arbeit → die Zusammenarbeit · das Amt → das Finanzamt

Wie du damit übst — how to use this

Level 1 shows you a bare noun. You choose the article, and then — this is the part that actually builds the skill — you name the rule that decided it. Recognising the gender is recall; naming the rule is transfer, and transfer is what works on a noun you have never seen. Level 2 takes the same nouns into the exam's real question: once you know the gender, can you put the right article word and the right adjective ending in front of it? Wrong answers are re-queued at the end of each round.

Level 2 — Artikelwort + Adjektivendung

This is where a gender error actually costs you points in the exam: the gender picks the case form, the article word decides whether the adjective is weak, mixed or strong. Watch the alle / viele pair — same slot, different ending, because of what stands in front.
