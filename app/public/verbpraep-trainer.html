The verb picks the preposition — the preposition picks the case.
Prepositional objects, the an/auf/in split, pronominal adverbs, questions and answers, word order.
The verb rules the sentence. It picks the preposition; the preposition picks the case.

German has several hundred verbs that carry a fixed preposition — warten auf, denken an, teilnehmen an. Nothing about the meaning tells you which preposition it will be, and English is an unreliable guide: to wait FOR is warten AUF, to think OF is denken AN, to ask FOR is bitten UM. What you can reason about is the case, because most prepositions only ever govern one.

The six-step method

  1. Learn the verb as a package of three

    Never store the verb alone. Store verb + preposition + case, plus one example sentence — exactly the way you store a noun with its article.

    warten auf + Akk. · denken an + Akk. · teilnehmen an + Dat. · sich erkundigen nach + Dat.

  2. Ask which case the preposition governs

    Most prepositions never change. Always dative: aus, bei, mit, nach, unter, von, vor, zu. Always accusative: durch, für, gegen, über, um.

    Wir sprechen mit dem Chef (Dat.) über das Projekt (Akk.).

  3. Only an, auf and in split — and the VERB decides, not wo?/wohin?

    This is the hardest part of the topic. The two-way logic you know from places does not apply. There is no movement anywhere; the verb simply fixes the case once and for all.

    teilnehmen an der Besprechung (Dat.) ← → denken an die Prüfung (Akk.)
    bestehen auf der Einhaltung (Dat.) ← → warten auf die Antwort (Akk.)
    bestehen in der Zusammensetzung (Dat.) ← → sich verlieben in den Kollegen (Akk.)

  4. Replacing: is the object a thing or a person?

    A thing becomes da(r) + Präposition. A person keeps the preposition and takes a personal pronoun. The -r- appears only when the preposition begins with a vowel.

    Ich warte auf den Urlaub. → Ich warte darauf.
    Ich warte auf meinen Chef. → Ich warte auf ihn.

  5. Asking: the same split, one step further

    A thing is asked about with wo(r) + Präposition. A person with Präposition + wen/wem — and the case comes from the preposition, not from the question.

    Worauf wartest du? — Auf das Ergebnis.
    Auf wen wartest du? — Auf die Handwerker.

  6. Good news: the da(r)-form carries no case

    da + Präposition is one frozen word. A dative verb and an accusative verb produce exactly the same replacement — so this one step is genuinely easy. The catch is that it gives you no practice in the case, which comes straight back the moment you use a real noun again.

    Ich warte auf die Antwort (Akk.) → Ich warte darauf.
    Der Vertrag beruht auf einer Grundlage (Dat.) → Er beruht darauf.
    Nimmst du an der Exkursion teil (Dat.)? → Nein, daran nehme ich nicht teil.

The one thing that trips everyone up

With a place, an · auf · in answer a question: wo? → dative, wohin? → accusative. With a prepositional object there is no place and no movement, so that question cannot be asked. The case is a property of the verb, listed in the dictionary, and it never varies. Ich nehme an der Besprechung teil is dative not because the meeting is a location, but because teilnehmen says so.
